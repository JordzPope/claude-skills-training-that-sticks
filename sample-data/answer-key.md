Everything in this file is fictional, like the Hallowmere Facilities sample data it marks.

# Answer key

The problems planted in each skill's example, so you can check what Claude finds when you say
"try it". Claude may find a little more than is listed here; a good run finds everything listed.
These answers sit outside the skill folders on purpose, so Claude can't read them.

## 01 Performance Gap Brief (`performance-gap-brief`)

Example: a draft brief asking for a FieldPro refresher for all 610 engineers.

1. The behaviour is written as labels ("take ownership of FieldPro", "become more digitally confident") with no observable action, situation or standard.
2. The gap rests on supervisors' opinion that "most engineers can't use the app", stated as fact, with lack of confidence as the cause. The brief's own data shows they can: notes are complete on 88% of planned jobs, and 597 of 610 passed the go-live practical check. Asked "could they do it if their life depended on it?", the answer is yes.
3. The causes training can't fix (a bonus that counts closed jobs, no signal in plant rooms and basements, supervisors accepting a phone call in place of notes) get no action or owner, and the brief still recommends a half-day refresher for everyone.
4. A named engineer is singled out as "the worst for missing notes", sent to the first session and given weekly checks.

## 02 On-the-Job Objectives (`on-the-job-objectives`)

Example: the provider's objectives and outline for Lead the Shift.

1. Objectives 1 to 3 use verbs nobody can watch: "Understand", "Be aware of", "Appreciate".
2. Objective 4, "Hold effective one-to-ones", has no situation and no standard, and "effective" can't be checked.
3. Objective 5 bundles three behaviours: giving feedback, coaching and managing absence.
4. Objective 6 (return-to-work conversations) is well written but has no practice in the outline: day 2 covers absence only through a policy talk, a system demo and a discussion.

## 03 Learning Impact Map (`learning-impact-map`)

Example: a draft map for Complaints That Close, with the course outline and the sponsor's notes.

1. Critical action A2 is knowledge restated as an action: "Advisers understand the complaints policy and the escalation routes".
2. Key result R2, "Customers feel listened to", has no measure, current figure or source, and its target is "Better than now".
3. Capability C4 (an hour on the history and structure of the complaints team) links to no critical action, result or goal.
4. R1 claims the course alone will cut reopened complaints from 1 in 5 to 1 in 20, but the sponsor's notes show 34 of 50 reopened complaints were repeat faults that depend on first-time fix in field operations. No other factor is named.

## 04 Transfer Risk Audit (`transfer-risk-audit`)

Example: the L&D coordinator's draft audit of the Asbestos Awareness refresher.

1. It rates people instead of the programme: "the older engineers, mainly at Hull and Sheffield" are labelled high risk (an age-based group), and the fix sends supervisors the names of the lowest-scoring 10%.
2. The whole manager row of the matrix says "Not needed", and manager-before is rated low risk, though it ranked as the most powerful point.
3. The work environment isn't assessed, though the facts show registers are PDFs FieldPro can't link to and most phones can't open, reactive jobs leave no time for a register check, and 9 of 14 near misses involved no register check.
4. 100% completion and a 92% average quiz score are treated as proof of use at work, the LMS marks people "competent" on a pass, and the trainer signs the record instead of a qualified person under the organisation's procedure.

## 05 Manager Pre-Course Brief (`manager-pre-course-brief`)

Example: an L&D business partner's draft email and conversation guide for Lead the Shift.

1. The course is framed as a punishment: the supervisor is going because their team's absence is the worst in the region and there have been two complaints about their one-to-ones, and it's "your chance to get on top of it before it goes any further".
2. It lists eight course topics (8 of the 15 minutes) instead of one or two things to try at work, with no if-then plan and no first real situation.
3. The manager offers no support or time: the supervisor must get ahead before the course and catch up after, and practice "will have to fit around their normal workload".
4. There's no review date, and the review is the year-end appraisal, where the manager will "score how well they've applied it" in a people management rating.

## 06 Course Joining Note (`course-joining-note`)

Example: the joining note for the FieldPro rollout, Sheffield group.

1. The weekday and date disagree: "Tuesday 18 January 2027" is a Monday.
2. There's no expectation of use at work: it never says every job goes through FieldPro from 1 February and paper job sheets stop, or that supervisors will check each engineer's first jobs.
3. The 40 minutes of pre-work is "best on a laptop" and to be done "at home in your own time", but engineers have no work laptops.
4. Adjustments are left until arrival, too late to arrange, and learners are asked to email "details of your condition so we can keep them on file".

## 07 Sponsor Message (`sponsor-message`)

Example: a draft message from the Head of Customer Operations for Complaints That Close.

1. It's full of hype: "a world-class programme for a world-class team", "the most exciting programme", "will revolutionise", "the best customer team in the industry".
2. The figure is wrong: "nearly half" of closed complaints are said to be reopened, against the source figure of 1 in 5.
3. It promises a monthly league table of every adviser's reopened complaints, ranking named people.
4. It threatens: anyone whose reopen rate hasn't come down by the summer "will be picked up in their performance review".

## 08 Real Work Brief (`real-work-brief`)

Example: a trainer's rough notes for the Asbestos Awareness refresher. Draft-only skill: Claude lists these, then drafts with each one fixed.

1. It assumes everyone does reactive jobs, but the cleaning and security site supervisors on the course don't, and there's no other task for them.
2. Learners are asked to photograph the client's asbestos register and the site contact sheet (which holds personal contact details) and send them on WhatsApp.
3. Photos are due on 15 March, after both sessions (9 and 11 March).
4. Anyone who spots the right stopping point is signed off "as asbestos competent for the year". Awareness training makes nobody competent, and sign-off stays with a qualified person.

## 09 Retrieval Practice Set (`retrieval-practice-set`)

Example: a trainer's draft knowledge check for the asbestos refresher.

1. Q2 is trivia (the year the UK banned all asbestos); knowing it changes nothing an engineer does on site.
2. Q4 is a recognition-only "all of the above" item; with Q2, two of seven items are recognition-only.
3. There's no spacing: the whole check is given once, on paper, in the last 15 minutes, then not again until next year.
4. Scores are recorded by name and sent to regional operations managers as a league table "so they can follow up with whoever is at the bottom".

## 10 Practice Scenarios (`practice-scenarios`)

Example: scenario cards for Complaints That Close.

1. Level 1 option d (tell the caller you won't be spoken to like that and end the call) is a wrong answer no adviser would choose with a frustrated caller. It isn't a real mistake.
2. Every piece of feedback is "Correct, well done!" or "Incorrect, try again": no consequence, no reason.
3. Level 3 is no harder in structure than Level 1 (one decision, every fact given, one right letter), with no grey area, and a defensible answer is marked wrong.
4. Level 2 makes the caller's age the source of difficulty ("Like a lot of older callers she gets confused and repeats herself").

## 11 Session Plan Rework (`session-plan-rework`)

Example: a half-day FieldPro session for engineers.

1. Telling dominates: about 110 minutes of tour, walkthrough and video against 25 minutes of practice and 15 of retrieval, with two telling blocks over 20 minutes and the first hands-on use at 11:25.
2. The 09:30 block sorts engineers into visual, auditory and kinaesthetic learners (learning styles), so only "kinaesthetic" engineers use the app.
3. The only practice is an invented job ("Replace kettle, Head Office kitchen") unlike the reactive client-site jobs engineers do. No real-work block.
4. The close is questions and an LMS feedback form: no action plan for first use at go-live, no handover to supervisors with a review date.

## 12 Transfer Action Plan (`transfer-action-plan`)

Example: the action plan page in the Lead the Shift workbook.

1. The worked example and the blank page are goals with no if-then structure ("Give my team more feedback", "Be more confident"), with no situation and nothing anyone could see.
2. There's no first use within days: "Over the next quarter", "Ongoing", "When the opportunity comes up".
3. Nothing asks what might get in the way or what the learner will do about it.
4. The page goes to the trainer and the manager and is reviewed "at your annual performance review": no dated review soon, and the plan stops belonging to the learner.

## 13 Spaced Follow-Up Plan (`spaced-follow-up-plan`)

Example: a follow-up plan for Lead the Shift, cohort 3.

1. It's front-loaded: five of six touchpoints fall in the first week or so, then nothing for about 11 weeks, so return-to-work conversations (two or three a quarter) get no touchpoint after week one.
2. There's no retrieval or practice: every touchpoint re-sends or recaps material.
3. Line managers are never involved, though they already hold regular one-to-ones; L&D owns every touchpoint.
4. In week 12, a named LMS report of who opened the materials goes to two directors "so they can decide who repeats the course".

## 14 Reinforcement Nudges (`reinforcement-nudges`)

Example: draft FieldPro nudges. Message 1 is the good one.

1. Message 2 only reminds and thanks; it asks for nothing at work.
2. Message 3 runs to about 250 words with five asks, including a 25-minute module that needs a depot PC: impossible on a phone in a van.
3. Message 4 threatens and guilt-trips: a weekly list of named engineers who skip recording parts, and "don't be the one who lets your depot down".
4. Message 5 opens "URGENT: action needed today" for a tip with no deadline.

## 15 Job Aid (`job-aid`)

Example: an Asbestos Awareness refresher card.

1. There's no trigger at the top: it opens with "About asbestos" and never says when to pick it up.
2. Side 1 is course content: about 190 words on history, the ban, fibre types and diseases.
3. The safety call is left to the engineer ("you can usually carry on with care" if it looks in good condition), the organisation's asbestos procedure is never named as the authority, and the learning designer owns it.
4. Colour carries the meaning: the action column is red, amber and green blocks with no text, which fails colour-blind engineers and black-and-white prints.

## 16 Practice Challenges (`practice-challenges`)

Example: a trainer's rough notes for Complaints That Close. Draft-only skill: Claude lists these, then drafts with each one fixed.

1. Week 1 adds five minutes to every complaint call against a 6-minute handle-time target reviewed daily, with no manager agreement, and runs far over 15 minutes a week.
2. Week 3 asks advisers to take a stage 2 complaint to resolution, but advisers pass stage 2 complaints to the resolution team.
3. Week 4 is a 500-word reflection emailed to the trainer, with no real work.
4. A named leaderboard on the team wallboard next to handle-time figures, the top three named at the huddle, vouchers, and bonus points for compliments advisers can't control.

## 17 Manager Follow-Up Guide (`manager-follow-up-guide`)

Example: a follow-up sheet for regional operations managers after Lead the Shift.

1. The first day-7 questions are leading or closed ("You've been doing weekly one-to-ones... haven't you?", "Did you use the feedback model?").
2. When a supervisor hasn't held a one-to-one yet, the sheet blames them and notes it for their development review, and never asks what got in the way.
3. Feedback is a label instead of SBI: "You're a natural with your team now. Keep it up."
4. After day 60 each supervisor is scored 1 to 5 and the scores go to the people team by name for the half-year review.

## 18 Observation Guide (`observation-guide`)

Example: a team leader's call listening form after Complaints That Close.

1. A 1 to 5 score on every line, averaged, with a 3.5 pass mark and a refresher for anyone below it.
2. Vague traits instead of behaviours: "Sounds confident", "Shows a professional attitude", "Takes ownership".
3. A personality judgement: "is this adviser naturally suited to complaints work, or more of a process person?"
4. It's used as a performance record: saved in the adviser's HR folder, with averages feeding quarterly reviews and probation decisions.

## 19 Peer Practice Circle (`peer-practice-circle`)

Example: an L&D business partner's rough notes after Contract Conversations. Draft-only skill: Claude lists these, then drafts with each one fixed.

1. All 12 contract managers in one circle; four to six works.
2. The Director of Operations chairs and will note "who contributes and who doesn't" for year-end reviews.
3. Meetings at 6pm, outside work time.
4. A named person is left off the invite because of a health detail.

## 20 Barrier Removal Plan (`barrier-removal-plan`)

Example: a draft barrier plan for the FieldPro rollout.

1. Row 1 gives the jobs-per-day target a training fix owned by the L&D coordinator; the real fix and owner sit with whoever sets the target.
2. Row 2 blames a group by age ("Older engineers at Hull and Sheffield are resistant to new technology"), with no work condition behind it.
3. Row 3 has a real barrier (no signal on hospital sites) with a vague fix ("Look into it"), "Ongoing" for a date and no sign it's fixed.
4. Row 4 names a supervisor and blames him; the work condition is that paper job sheets are still accepted, a decision for the process owner.

## 21 Transfer Evaluation Plan (`transfer-evaluation-plan`)

Example: the evaluation plan for the Asbestos Awareness refresher 2027.

1. It stops at completion and satisfaction: the "Level 3 Behaviour" rows are LMS completion and a relevance rating, and nothing at LTEM tiers 5 to 7 measures what engineers do on site, though the FieldPro register tick and supervisors' site checklists exist.
2. Measures have no baseline (stop-work reports, rating, quiz, completion), though last year's refresher could supply them.
3. A business result (contract SLA breaches, about 40 a month to 30) is claimed with no link to checking the register or stopping work, and no other factors named.
4. Quiz scores by named engineer go into the monthly operations report "so the regional operations managers can see who is falling behind".

## 22 Application Survey (`application-survey`)

Example: a FieldPro feedback survey.

1. It's a happy sheet: satisfaction, the trainer, the room, confidence and a recommend score, and nothing on which FieldPro behaviours people use, how often, what got in the way or what changed.
2. Q7 is leading: "How much will FieldPro improve the way you record job notes?" with no "no change" option.
3. Q1 collects employee numbers to match answers to FieldPro usage, so it isn't anonymous.
4. The timing is wrong: a QR code at the end of each session, before anyone has used FieldPro at work. Day 60 after go-live is early March 2027.

## 23 Success Case Interviews (`success-case-interviews`)

Example: a Lead the Shift success stories write-up.

1. Managers nominated all the interviewees, all from the success end: no screening survey and no interviews with the least successful, so "why some haven't changed" rests on opinion.
2. Finding 3 fails the evidence standard: "I'd say it's halved" with no figure, period or record, stretched to "Lead the Shift has halved absence". (Finding 2, checked against job records, passes.)
3. Finding 1 turns five of six interviews into "around 83% of Lead the Shift graduates".
4. Finding 4 quotes a named supervisor at a named depot with no consent recorded.

## 24 Learning Impact Report (`learning-impact-report`)

Example: the impact report for Complaints That Close.

1. It claims attribution the evidence can't carry: the fall in reopened complaints is credited wholly to the programme, though Appendix B shows a June CRM change, 14 new advisers and two extra team leaders in the same period.
2. The 540% ROI has no method: no isolation, no unit value or source, no sum, and costs not fully loaded. It's repeated in the headline.
3. Survey figures ("91% of advisers") are 42 of 46 respondents out of 180 advisers, self-report presented as behaviour across everyone.
4. Appendix C ranks 12 advisers by employee number and the report decides they'll redo the course.
