# Asbestos: quick reference card (draft v0.3)

Goes with: Asbestos Awareness refresher 2026 · For: all engineers and site supervisors · Format: printed A5 card, two-sided, kept in the van door pocket, plus a PDF on the LMS · Drafted by: learning designer, Monday 26 October 2026

## Side 1: About asbestos

Asbestos is a group of natural fibrous minerals that was used widely in British buildings for decades because it is strong, cheap and resists heat. Its use was banned in the UK in 1999, so any building built or refurbished before 2000 can contain it. You may find it in ceiling tiles, pipe lagging, boiler and heater panels, textured coatings on walls and ceilings, floor tiles, cement roof sheets, gutters and downpipes, toilet cisterns, fire doors and old electrical panels. There are three main types, chrysotile (white), amosite (brown) and crocidolite (blue), but you can't tell the type, or whether a material contains asbestos at all, just by looking at it. When asbestos is damaged or disturbed it releases fibres that can be breathed in. The diseases they cause, including mesothelioma, asbestosis and lung cancer, usually take 15 to 60 years to appear, which is why asbestos still causes thousands of deaths in Great Britain every year. Maintenance trades such as electricians, plumbers, joiners and heating engineers are among the people most at risk, because their work disturbs the fabric of buildings.

## Side 2: Before you start

1. Check the building's age on the job in FieldPro.
2. If it was built before 2000, open the site's asbestos register (in the FieldPro site record, or the site logbook at reception).
3. Find the area you'll be working in and check it against the table.

| What the register says about where you'll work | |
| --- | --- |
| Asbestos, or presumed asbestos, and your work could disturb it | (red block) |
| Surveyed, and no asbestos found | (green block) |
| You can't find the register, or it doesn't cover where you'll work | (amber block) |
| You find a material that might be asbestos and isn't on the register | (amber block) |

On the printed card the right-hand column is a solid coloured block with no text, to keep the card clean. Key at the foot of the card: red, don't start · amber, see the note · green, carry on.

**Amber note:** If you can't find the register, or you come across something that might be asbestos, have a look at its condition. If it looks in good condition and your work will only touch it lightly, you can usually carry on with care. If it's damaged or crumbling, or you'd need to drill, cut or sand it, stop and call your supervisor.

## If you think you've disturbed asbestos

- Stop work straight away.
- Keep other people out of the area.
- Don't try to clean up any dust or debris.
- Tell your engineering supervisor and log it in the near-miss app.
