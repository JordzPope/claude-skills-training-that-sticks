# Lead the Shift: what it changed (draft write-up for the Director of Operations)

Prepared by: L&D business partner
Date: 21 October 2026

## Background

Lead the Shift is Hallowmere's first-line management programme. 64 people took part between March and June 2026: 46 engineering supervisors and 18 contact centre team leaders, most of them promoted from the tools or the phones. It ran as four half-day workshops on one-to-ones, giving feedback and absence conversations, and each person agreed an action plan with their manager at the end.

## How we found the stories

We asked the four regional operations managers and the two contact centre managers to put forward the people in their teams who had made the biggest strides since the course. They nominated nine people, and we interviewed six of them on Teams in September: four engineering supervisors and two team leaders. Each interview took about 30 minutes, and we took notes as we went.

## What we found

**1. Regular one-to-ones are now the norm.** Five of the six people we spoke to now hold a monthly one-to-one with every member of their team. That means around 83% of Lead the Shift graduates have built the habit, up from almost none before the course.

**2. Feedback is landing.** One engineering supervisor described an engineer who had gone back to three jobs in a month after arriving without the right parts. The next morning the supervisor used the feedback model from module 2: what they'd seen on each job, the effect on the client and on the first-time fix figures, then asked what would help. They agreed the engineer would check the parts list with the planner the night before each job. The supervisor showed us the engineer's job records: no return visits for missing parts in the eight weeks since.

**3. Absence is down.** A contact centre team leader told us: "Since the course my team's absence has really dropped. I'd say it's halved." Lead the Shift has halved absence in the teams of the people who are using it.

**4. Supervisors feel ready for the hard conversations.** Priya, an engineering supervisor at our Hull depot, put it well: "For the first time I know what to say when someone rings in sick, and I don't dread Monday mornings any more."

## Why some people haven't changed

The managers told us the people who haven't changed much are mostly the ones who were never really bought in to being a supervisor. That's a motivation problem, and more training won't fix it.

## Recommendations

1. Run Lead the Shift for the 2027 intake of new supervisors and team leaders.
2. Use the stories above in the 2027 programme launch and in the next board report.
