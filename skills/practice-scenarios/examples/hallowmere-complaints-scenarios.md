# Complaints That Close: practice scenarios (draft)

From: learning designer
For: customer advisers at the Leeds contact centre, afternoon of the Complaints That Close day
Format: printed cards worked in pairs, then a trainer debrief. About 15 minutes per level.

## Level 1: Leaking again

A receptionist at a council office calls. An engineer fixed a leaking radiator in their main meeting room yesterday, and it's leaking again this morning. The room is booked for a meeting with councillors at 2pm, and the caller is frustrated.

What do you do first?

a) Apologise, ask what's happening now and what they need by 2pm
b) Book a new engineer visit straight away from the job history
c) Tell the caller the engineer's notes say the job was completed and tested
d) Tell the caller you won't be spoken to like that and end the call

Correct answer: a

Feedback: a) Correct, well done! b), c) and d) Incorrect, try again.

## Level 2: The hallway lights

An 81-year-old resident of a council sheltered housing block that Hallowmere maintains calls the contact centre. Like a lot of older callers she gets confused and repeats herself, so this scenario is about staying patient. She says the lights in the communal hallway have been off for three days and residents are worried about falls at night. The queue board shows 14 callers waiting.

Decision 1: What do you do first?

a) Confirm which hallway and since when, then check the job history with her
b) Tell her there's a job on the system for the lights, so they should be working
c) Raise a new job and tell her an engineer will be out within the contract time
d) Ask her to get the scheme manager to call in, as they can report it properly

Correct answer: a

Decision 2: The job history shows an engineer attended on Tuesday and closed the job as "fixed, faulty sensor replaced". What do you do?

a) Reopen the original job, link this call to it, and agree a visit time with her today
b) Raise a separate new job so the first one stays closed
c) Explain that the job shows as fixed and ask her to check the lights again tonight
d) Email the planners and tell her someone will be in touch

Correct answer: a

Feedback: Decision 1: a) Correct, well done! Others: Incorrect, try again. Decision 2: a) Correct, well done! Others: Incorrect, try again.

## Level 3: The sticking door

An estates manager at a hospital calls. The automatic door at the outpatients entrance has stuck open three times this month, and an engineer has been out each time. A replacement part is on order and due next Thursday. The estates manager wants it fixed today and says they'll raise it at the next contract review.

What do you do?

a) Own the repeat visits, confirm the part date and offer a check visit before then
b) Explain that the part is on order and nothing more can be done until Thursday
c) Apologise and promise an engineer will have the door fixed today
d) Transfer the call to the contract manager, as it's now a contract issue

Correct answer: a

Feedback: a) Correct, well done! b), c) and d) Incorrect, try again.
