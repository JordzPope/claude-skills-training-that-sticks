# Asbestos Awareness refresher: knowledge check (draft)

From: trainer, Hallowmere Facilities L&D team
For: all engineers and site supervisors, at the annual Asbestos Awareness refresher (half a day, run at each depot)

## When it runs

The whole check is handed out on paper in the last 15 minutes of the refresher session. Engineers and supervisors take it again at next year's refresher.

## How results are used

The L&D coordinator enters everyone's score against their name. Each regional operations manager then gets a league table for their depots, highest score first, so they can follow up with whoever is at the bottom.

## Questions

**1.** You're sent on a reactive job to replace a light fitting in a classroom ceiling at a primary school built in 1972. The job sheet has no asbestos information on it. What do you do before you lift a ceiling tile? (Write your answer.)

Model answer: Check the site's asbestos register or survey for that room. If you can't get hold of it, or it doesn't cover the ceiling void, leave the tile where it is and call your supervisor.

Feedback: A school of this age can have asbestos in ceiling tiles and voids. The register shows what's known to be there and where, so you check it before you disturb anything.

**2.** In which year was the use of all types of asbestos banned in the UK?

a) 1985
b) 1999
c) 2005
d) 2012

Answer: b

Feedback:
a) 1985 is when blue and brown asbestos were banned. White asbestos was still in use after that.
b) The UK banned the use of all types of asbestos in 1999.
c) Too late: the full ban was already in place by then.
d) 2012 is the year of the current Control of Asbestos Regulations, long after the ban.

**3.** You're drilling a fixing hole in a plant room wall and notice the board behind the plasterboard looks grey and fibrous. The drill bit is about a centimetre in. What do you do next?

a) Finish the hole quickly, then vacuum up the dust so nobody else breathes it in
b) Stop work, keep other people out of the plant room and report it to your supervisor
c) Damp the area down with a spray bottle, then carry on carefully
d) Break off a small piece and bag it so it can be sent for testing

Answer: b

Feedback:
a) An ordinary vacuum cleaner spreads fine fibres into the air, and finishing the hole disturbs more of the board.
b) Stopping limits what's disturbed, keeping people out protects them, and your supervisor arranges for it to be checked and cleaned up safely.
c) Damping down is a method for people trained and equipped to work with asbestos. Carrying on still disturbs the board.
d) Only people trained to take samples should do it. Breaking a piece off releases fibres.

**4.** Which of these can contain asbestos?

a) Pipe lagging
b) Ceiling tiles
c) Floor tiles
d) All of the above

Answer: d

Feedback:
a) Pipe lagging can contain asbestos, but so can the others.
b) Ceiling tiles can contain asbestos, but so can the others.
c) Floor tiles can contain asbestos, but so can the others.
d) Asbestos was used in many building materials, including all three of these.

**5.** At a council office built in 1986, the client's site manager tells you, "There's no asbestos in this building, just crack on." The job means cutting into a partition wall, and you haven't seen the asbestos register. What do you say and do? (Write your answer.)

Model answer: Thank them and ask to see the asbestos register or survey for that area. If they can't show you one that covers the wall, don't start any work that disturbs it, and call your supervisor to take it up with the client.

Feedback: The register is the record of what has been found and where. The site manager may sincerely believe the building is clear and still be wrong, so you go by the register.

**6.** Write down what you do, in order, the moment you think you've disturbed asbestos.

Model answer: Stop work straight away. Keep other people away from the area. Leave the clean-up alone. Tell your supervisor and follow their instructions.

Feedback: Stopping first limits how much is released. Keeping people away, and leaving the clean-up to people trained for it, stops the fibres spreading further.

**7.** You arrive at a hospital to fix a leaking pipe in a basement corridor. The pipe lagging next to the leak is labelled "Asbestos: do not disturb". What do you do?

a) Work carefully around the lagging so the leak is fixed today
b) Isolate the water, then call your supervisor to replan the job around the lagging
c) Wrap the lagging in a bin bag to protect it, then fix the leak
d) Isolate the water, then close the job in FieldPro as "no access"

Answer: b

Feedback:
a) Working on the pipe next to the lagging risks disturbing it, however careful you are.
b) The label tells you the job needs planning around the asbestos. Your supervisor can arrange that, and the hospital still gets its leak fixed.
c) Wrapping it means handling it, which can disturb it.
d) Closing the job as "no access" hides the real reason, so the leak comes back as a complaint and the next engineer walks into the same situation.
