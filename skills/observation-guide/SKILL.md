# Observation Guide

Produces a short guide to one task: what good looks like as behaviours someone can see or hear, space to note what happened, and how to turn it into SBI feedback straight after. Or checks a form someone already uses. It gives every manager the same picture of good, so feedback after training is specific and fair.

This guide exists for feedback and learning. It holds no scores or ratings, it signs no one off as competent, and it must never be used as evidence in a performance, capability or disciplinary process. Every guide this skill writes tells the person being observed exactly that.

## When to use

- "What should team leaders listen for on a call after our complaints training?"
- "Write a what-good-looks-like guide for supervisors watching engineers use the new job app."
- "Our new managers have done the one-to-ones module. What should their manager look for when they sit in on one?"
- "Check our call listening form before team leaders start using it."
- "Turn these course objectives into things a manager can actually watch for."

Wrong tool for rating someone, deciding probation, signing off competence or handling a performance concern: those stay with people, under your organisation's own procedures. For check-in questions after a course, use the Manager Follow-Up Guide; to write the objectives first, use On-the-Job Objectives.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the task to observe and the programme it came from, with the objectives or the steps taught. [one task; if several are given, start with the first and offer the rest]
2. **Essential:** whether the task is safety-critical or regulated. [no]
3. Who observes and how: in person, a live or recorded call, a site visit, or reviewing finished work such as job notes. [the learner's line manager, live, agreed with the learner in advance]
4. Any procedure or standard the task follows. [the steps as taught on the course]
5. When observations happen. [one at about day 14 to 30 after the course, or after go-live for a system rollout; a second about a month later]

Never ask for learners' names, call recordings, customer details, past scores, health information or protected characteristics. The guide describes the task, so it needs nothing about any one person. If the user includes such details, leave them out and say so.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any attached objectives, procedure or existing form. If they've given an existing observation form, call listening sheet or checklist, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected guide.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Split the task into three to six moments in the order they happen (for a complaint call: opening, finding out, agreeing the fix, closing). Under each, write two to four behaviours, each starting with a verb and describing something seen or heard: "Repeats the customer's main concern back before offering a fix." Where the user has a procedure, take the standard from it, in the shape of a Mager-style objective (the behaviour, the conditions, the standard). Where there is none (how many photos count, how full a job note must be), write the behaviour without a number and mark the standard [to agree with the process owner].
4. Test every behaviour: would two observers watching the same work agree whether it happened? If not, rewrite it. Replace traits and attitudes ("confident", "professional attitude", "takes ownership", "good rapport") with the behaviour that would show them. Delete anything about personality, accent, appearance, age or how someone "comes across", and say what you deleted.
5. Allow more than one right way. Where other approaches meet the same standard, say so ("in their own words", "in any order"). Keep exact wording only where a procedure or regulator requires it, and name that procedure.
6. Give each moment a notes column for facts: what was said or done, and when. Add no score, grade, rating scale, pass mark, overall verdict, or tick column that would work as one.
7. Write the feedback section for straight after, or within [1] working day, in private. The learner speaks first: "How did that go? What would you do the same next time?" Then two or three SBI points from the notes, at least one on something that went well. **Situation:** when and where. **Behaviour:** what they did or said. **Impact:** what happened as a result. Close with one thing the learner chooses to try next. Give a worked SBI line for each moment, with [placeholders].
8. Write the "How this guide is used" box for the top of the guide, in plain words the learner reads before being observed:
   - It's for feedback and learning, with no score, rating or sign-off.
   - Everyone doing this task is observed with the same guide.
   - The learner sees the guide first and knows when the observation will happen.
   - The learner is asked whether any reasonable adjustment would help, for the observation or the task, arranged through [the organisation's process].
   - The notes go to the learner after the feedback. They stay off the personnel file and are never used in appraisal, probation, capability or disciplinary decisions.
   - L&D sees only counts and themes (which behaviours most people found hard), with no names.
9. If the task is safety-critical or regulated, add a box above everything else: competence assessment and sign-off stay with [the qualified assessor] under [the organisation's procedure], and this guide plays no part in them. If the observer sees unsafe work, they stop the job and follow [the safety procedure] at once.
10. Run the Quality checks below and fix what fails.
11. Present the guide, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: that each behaviour matches what the course taught and your procedure, who keeps the notes and for how long, how adjustments are requested, and whether the task is safety-critical.
12. Offer a .docx, a one-page pocket version for observers, the Job Aid skill to turn the same behaviours into a checklist for learners, or the Manager Follow-Up Guide.

## Output format

**Observation guide: [task], after [programme]**
For [observer role] observing [learner role]. For feedback only.

1. **Safety-critical task** box (only when needed).
2. **How this guide is used**: the six points from step 8.
3. **What good looks like**
   | Moment | Watch or listen for | Notes (what you saw or heard) |
   | [moment] | [behaviour, one per line] | |
4. **Feedback straight after**: opening questions for the learner, one SBI example per moment, and one thing to try next.
5. **What goes to L&D**: counts and themes, no names.

The table fits on one page; two pages at most in all.

## Try it

`examples/hallowmere-complaint-call-listening-form.md` is a call listening form a contact centre team leader drafted after Complaints That Close, a customer course at Hallowmere Facilities, a fictional 1,400-person facilities services firm, with problems planted in it. When the user says "try it", "show me" or "use the example", read that file, run the check job on it and list every problem you find before writing the corrected guide. The answers are in `sample-data/answer-key.md` in the pack's GitHub repo (https://github.com/JordzPope/claude-skills-training-that-sticks), outside this skill, so the user can check your work. Use the example's facts only for this; never carry them into a draft of the user's own case.

## Quality checks

- Every behaviour is something two observers watching the same work would agree happened or didn't; none is a trait or attitude such as "confident", "professional attitude" or "good rapport".
- Nothing judges personality, accent, appearance, age or how the person "comes across".
- There is no rating scale, score, grade, pass mark or overall verdict anywhere, and no tick column that works as one.
- Nothing says or suggests the notes go on a personnel file or feed appraisal, probation, capability or disciplinary decisions, and the "How this guide is used" box says in plain words that they don't.
- The guide is the same for everyone doing the task and asks about reasonable adjustments before the observation.
- For a safety-critical or regulated task, the guide leaves competence sign-off with the qualified assessor under the organisation's procedure and says what to do if unsafe work is seen.
- Every SBI example has a situation, an observable behaviour and an impact, and the learner speaks first.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the plans, nudges and reports. Managers do the coaching, and a person makes every call about someone's skill. Claude drafts what to watch for; the manager watches and gives the feedback. Any call on whether someone is competent belongs to a person, under the organisation's procedure, and this guide is no evidence for it.
- Observation notes about a named person are personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018. Keep them out of Claude, give them to the learner, keep them only as long as the feedback needs, and share counts and themes with L&D. Follow the ICO's guidance and your organisation's AI policy.
- Use the same guide for everyone doing the task. Never infer or mention protected characteristics, and remove wording about accent, appearance or personality, which carries bias. The Equality Act 2010 requires reasonable adjustments for disabled people, and that covers being observed as well as the task itself.
- For safety-critical or regulated work (the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and any sector regulator's rules), competence assessment stays with the organisation's qualified assessor. Check the current versions of the laws named here on GOV.UK and the ICO website, and your own procedures. This is a draft guide, never legal advice.
- Behaviours, standards and procedure names come from the user or stay as placeholders.
