# Manager Follow-Up Guide

Produces the guide a line manager uses for three short check-ins after a course, at day 7, 30 and 60: GROW coaching questions for each, SBI prompts for feedback on what they've seen, and a plan for when the learner hasn't had a chance to use it. Or checks a guide someone already has. L&D writes it once per programme and every manager of every learner gets the same guide, so follow-up happens without anyone being chased.

## When to use

- "Managers keep asking what they should say after someone's been on a course."
- "Give our team leaders questions for a week after the customer training, then a month after."
- "Write a day 7, 30 and 60 follow-up guide for managers of people on the new systems rollout."
- "Check this post-course check-in sheet before it goes out with the programme."
- "How should a supervisor follow up after the first-line management course?"

Wrong tool for the conversation before the course (use the Manager Pre-Course Brief), for watching someone do the task and giving feedback on it (use the Observation Guide), or for fixing what blocks a whole team (use the Barrier Removal Plan). No skill in this pack rates or signs off a learner.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the programme and the two to four things learners should now do at work. Paste the objectives, the session outline or the learners' action plan. [if only a course title is given, Claude proposes behaviours and marks them as proposed]
2. **Essential:** the learners and their managers, by role (for example "customer advisers; their team leaders").
3. The date the course ends. [check-ins counted in calendar days from the last session; left as "Day 7" and so on if no date]
4. How managers and learners already meet. [a 15-minute slot added to an existing one-to-one, in work time]
5. What learners leave the course with. [an action plan with one or two if-then plans]
6. Whether any task covered is safety-critical or regulated. [no]

Never ask for learners' names, quiz scores, appraisal ratings or health details. The guide is written once for every manager on the programme, so it needs nothing about any one person. If the user includes them, work with roles and say so.

## Steps

Numbers in square brackets in these steps and the Quality checks are defaults. Use the user's own figure if they give one; otherwise use the default without asking.

1. Read what the user has given, including any attached objectives, action plan template or session outline. If they've given an existing follow-up guide, checklist or email to managers, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list every problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected guide.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If the user says "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Write two to four behaviours the check-ins focus on. Each is something the learner does at work, in a situation that comes up in their job: "Opens each one-to-one by asking what's gone well since the last one." Rewrite "understand" or "be aware of" objectives as behaviours and mark them as proposed.
4. Set the purpose of each check-in. **Day 7:** the first try (has a chance come up, what happened, when's the next one). **Day 30:** making it routine (what's working, what to adjust, what's in the way). **Day 60:** habit and results (what's changed in the work, what they'll keep doing, what to learn next). Spread them over two months on purpose: a study of 10,514 people in real workplace training (Kim and colleagues, 2019) found the best gap between returns to a skill grows with how long people need to keep it. Keep each check-in to 10 to 15 minutes and fold it into a meeting that already happens.
5. If managers held no conversation before the course, add one day 7 question on what the learner hoped to get from it. Broad and Newstrom's transfer matrix ranks the manager's part before training as the most powerful point of all, so suggest the Manager Pre-Course Brief for the next cohort.
6. Write the GROW questions for each check-in, one or two per stage:
   - **Goal:** what the learner wants to get better at or try next. The learner sets it; the manager asks.
   - **Reality:** the last time the situation came up, what they did and what happened. Ask for a specific example.
   - **Options:** two or three ways to try it, or to try it differently. The learner suggests first; the manager adds ideas after.
   - **Way forward** (sometimes called Will): one action, when, and what the manager will do to help.
   Fit every question to the programme: for a systems rollout, ask about the last job closed in the system; for a management course, the last one-to-one. At day 30 and day 60, open by picking up the action agreed last time. Every question starts with what, how, when, which or "tell me". Rewrite any question a person could answer yes or no, and any that suggests the answer.
7. Write the SBI feedback prompts, one per behaviour, for when the manager has seen or heard the learner use it. **Situation:** when and where. **Behaviour:** what they did or said, as a fact anyone present could confirm. **Impact:** what happened as a result for the customer, the team or the work. Add a version for something that went less well: the learner says how it went first, then one SBI point and one thing to try next. Rewrite any line that labels the person ("you're a natural", "you lack confidence") as the behaviour behind it.
8. Write the "hasn't had a chance" section. Baldwin and Ford's model puts the work environment alongside the learner and the course as one of three inputs to transfer, so start with the work. Ask what got in the way (time, tools, targets, instructions that clash, no suitable task), agree one real chance to use it within [2] weeks that the manager will make happen, and pass anything the manager can't fix to [L&D contact] for the Barrier Removal Plan. Say plainly that nothing is recorded against the learner.
9. Write the notes rule. The learner keeps their own notes. The manager writes down only the action and date they agreed. L&D asks managers for counts and themes (how many check-ins happened, the common barriers) with no names, scores or ratings.
10. If any task is safety-critical or regulated, add a box: the check-ins are coaching, competence sign-off stays with [the qualified assessor] under [the organisation's procedure], and any concern about safe working goes to that procedure the same day.
11. Add one day 7 question on whether anything would make it easier to use at work (a different format for the job aid, more time, equipment), with a pointer to [the organisation's reasonable adjustments process].
12. Run the Quality checks below and fix what fails.
13. Present the guide, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the check-in dates, that the behaviours match what the course taught, who managers contact in L&D, and whether a 15-minute slot fits the way these teams work.
14. Offer a .docx, a one-page card for managers, the Observation Guide for a behaviour the manager can watch, or the Barrier Removal Plan for what the check-ins turn up.

## Output format

**Manager follow-up guide: [programme]**
For [manager role] with each [learner role]. Course ends [date].

1. **What they've learned to do**
   | Behaviour at work | When it comes up in the job | What they left the course with |
2. **How to use this guide**: four or five sentences: three check-ins of 10 to 15 minutes in work time, the learner does most of the talking, it's coaching with no ratings, and what to write down.
3. **Day 7 check-in** ([date]): the first try
   | GROW stage | Ask | Listen for |
   | Goal | | |
   | Reality | | |
   | Options | | |
   | Way forward | | |
4. **Day 30 check-in** ([date]): making it routine. Same table, opening with last time's action.
5. **Day 60 check-in** ([date]): habit and results. Same table.
6. **Feedback on what you've seen (SBI)**: for each behaviour, one Situation, Behaviour, Impact example with [placeholders], then the version for when it went less well.
7. **If they haven't had a chance to use it**: three or four questions, what you can sort yourself, and who to pass the rest to.
8. **Notes, and what goes to L&D**.
9. **Safety-critical tasks** (only when needed).

Two pages at most.

## Quality checks

- Every question is open (what, how, when, which or "tell me"), none can be answered yes or no, and none suggests the answer.
- Each check-in has at least one question for each GROW stage, and the day 30 and day 60 check-ins open with the action agreed at the one before.
- The learner sets the goal at every check-in, and every way forward ends with one action, a date and what the manager will do.
- Nothing rates, scores or grades the learner: no scales, no "met / not met", and no link to appraisal, probation, performance review or pay.
- The "hasn't had a chance" section looks first at the work, gives the manager an action with a timescale, names who to pass unfixed barriers to, and records nothing against the learner.
- Every SBI example has a situation, a behaviour anyone present could confirm and an impact, with no labels about the person.
- Anything going to L&D is counts and themes with no names. Dates and contacts the user didn't give are marked as proposed and listed under Check before you use it.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: Claude drafts the plans, nudges and reports. Managers do the coaching, and a person makes every call about someone's skill. Claude drafts these questions; the manager holds each check-in and listens. Whether someone can do the job is a call for a person, and for safety-critical work it belongs to the qualified assessor under the organisation's procedure.
- Work with roles, never names. Check-in notes about a named learner are personal data under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018: keep them between the learner and the manager, share only counts and themes with L&D, and follow the ICO's guidance and your organisation's AI policy before pasting anything into Claude.
- Use one guide for every learner on the programme. Never infer or mention protected characteristics. The Equality Act 2010 covers reasonable adjustments for disabled learners, including when they use what they learned at work, so point to your adjustments process. If a performance concern comes up, it goes through your organisation's own process with HR, kept apart from these check-ins.
- The Equality Act 2010, UK GDPR, the Data Protection Act 2018 and, for safety-critical work, the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 may apply. Check the current versions on GOV.UK and the ICO website, and your own policies. This is a draft guide, never legal advice.
- Behaviours, dates, contacts and timescales come from the user or stay as placeholders.
