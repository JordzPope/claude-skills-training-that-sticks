# Performance Gap Brief

Turns "we need training on X" into a brief of one or two pages: the behaviour the work needs, the gap between that and what happens now, the evidence, and what's causing it. Or checks a brief someone has already written. It tells the person who asked, before anyone books a course, whether training will close the gap, close part of it, or leave it where it is.

## When to use

- "Operations want a refresher on the new job app for every engineer. Can you scope it before I say yes?"
- "Our director wants customer service training for the whole contact centre by March."
- "How do I push back on a training request without just saying no?"
- "Check this needs analysis before it goes to the Head of Operations."
- "Is this even a training problem?"

Wrong tool for one person's performance, which is a conversation between them and their manager under your performance process. Once training is agreed, use On-the-Job Objectives to write what the course should change.

## Inputs

Ask once for anything missing. Defaults in brackets.

1. **Essential:** the request as it arrived: who asked, for which group, and what they asked for. An email, call notes or a ticket pasted in is fine.
2. **Essential:** what's going wrong at work: the result that's off (a measure, complaints, incidents, audit findings) and what people do or don't do. [taken from the request; anything missing becomes a question for the requester]
3. The group: roles, how many, where they work, and whether the gap shows up everywhere or in some teams, sites or shifts. [the roles named in the request, all sites]
4. What has been tried already, including any past training on this. [nothing tried; past training unknown]
5. The deadline or event behind the request (a contract review, an audit, a go-live). [none]

Never ask for learners' names, individual scores, appraisal ratings, absence or health records, or protected characteristics. The brief is about a group doing a task. If the user pastes data about named people, set the names aside, work from the pattern across the group and say you've done so.

## Steps

1. Read what the user has given, including attachments. If they've given an existing brief or needs analysis, this is a check job: run the Quality checks on it, list each problem with where it sits and why it matters, then write a corrected brief.
2. Ask for missing essentials in one message, five questions at most, with the defaults shown. If they say "just draft it", use the defaults and list them as assumptions.
3. Name the result and the behaviour. Write the result the requester wants to move, with today's figure and its source. Then write the behaviour behind it: what someone in the role is seen or heard doing, in which situation, to what standard. Turn labels such as attitude, ownership, confidence and awareness into something a person could watch. If the request names a solution ("a two-day course", "a refresher"), park it until step 8.
4. Describe the gap: what happens now, how often, and where (everyone, some teams, some sites, new starters only). Tag every piece of evidence as **data** (a system report, an audit, a measure), **observation** (someone watched it happen) or **opinion** (someone says so). Where opinion is all there is, propose one quick way to get data and mark it proposed. Mager and Pipe also ask whether a gap is worth closing: say what this one costs (a contract target, safety, complaints, rework), and if it costs little, say so.
5. Test for a skill gap with Mager and Pipe's question: could people in the role do it if their life depended on it? Look for signs they can: they do it well in some situations, sites or weeks; they used to do it well; they passed a practical check. Answer yes, no or partly, with the evidence.
6. Sort the causes. Write each as a short statement with its type and its evidence, or "to test" plus how to test it (a 20-minute chat with three people in the role, a look at the system report, a shift alongside the team).
   - Causes training can fix: **knowledge** (they don't know what, why or when) and **skill** (they know but can't yet do it to standard). Check for something simpler before a course: a job aid when the task is rare, practice with feedback when they used to do it well.
   - Causes training can't fix: **expectations** (unclear, or nobody asks for it), **tools and information** (missing, slow, or out of reach where the work happens), **time** (no room in the job to do it properly), **consequences** (targets, bonuses or praise that reward the old way, or nothing happens either way) and **feedback** (people never find out how they're doing).

   Usually there's a mix. If step 5 answered yes, expect most causes in the second group.
7. Plan a fix for every cause training can't fix: the change that would remove it and who owns it, usually an operations lead, a line manager, IT or whoever sets the targets. Mark any owner you propose.
8. Give the verdict plainly, in one of three forms:
   - **Training will close this gap**: the causes are knowledge or skill.
   - **Training will close part of this gap**: say which other fixes must come first or alongside, because people go back to the workplace that produced the gap.
   - **This isn't a training problem**: no knowledge or skill cause was found. Name what will close the gap, who owns it, and what L&D can still offer (a job aid, a briefing for managers on the expectation).

   Then return to the solution the requester asked for, say whether it fits the verdict, and how to change it if it doesn't.
9. Set out how everyone will know the gap has closed: the measure, today's figure, the target and the date, plus the behaviour a manager would see. Figures come from the user or stay as placeholders.
10. Run the Quality checks and fix what fails.
11. Present the brief, then **Assumptions** and **Check before you use it**: the evidence and its sources, every cause marked "to test", the owners of fixes outside L&D, and whether the requester accepts the verdict.
12. Offer a .docx, a short reply to the requester explaining the verdict, or On-the-Job Objectives and Learning Impact Map if training is part of the answer.

## Output format

**Performance gap brief: [request or programme name]**
Requested by [role] for [roles, number, sites]. Prepared by [role], [date].

1. **The request**: [what was asked for, in the requester's words]
2. **The result to move**: [measure], now [figure, source], target [figure] by [date].
3. **The behaviour at work**

| What people in the role do | In which situation | To what standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [observable action] | [the trigger or task] | [how well, how often, how fast] |

4. **The gap**

| What happens now | Evidence and source | Data, observation or opinion | Where and how widespread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

5. **Could they do it if their life depended on it?** [Yes, no or partly], because [evidence].
6. **Causes**

| Cause | Type | Evidence, or how to test it | Training can fix it? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [statement] | [knowledge, skill, expectations, tools and information, time, consequences, feedback] | [source, or "to test: how"] | [yes or no] |

7. **Verdict**: [Training will close this gap / Training will close part of this gap / This isn't a training problem]. [Two or three sentences on why, and whether the requested solution fits.]
8. **What closes the gap**

| Action | Cause it fixes | Owner | By when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

9. **How we'll know**: [measure] from [figure] to [figure] by [date], from [source]. A manager would see [behaviour].

Two pages at most.

## Quality checks

- The behaviour is written as something a person could be seen or heard doing at work, with a situation and a standard; no label (attitude, ownership, confidence, awareness) stands in for it.
- Every statement about the gap names its source and is tagged data, observation or opinion.
- The life-depended-on-it question is answered yes, no or partly, with evidence, and the answer agrees with the cause table.
- Every cause has a type, and either evidence or a "to test" note saying how to test it.
- Every cause training can't fix has an action and an owner; a proposed owner is marked and listed under Check before you use it.
- The verdict uses one of the three wordings, and training is recommended only for causes typed knowledge or skill.
- The brief describes a group: no learner is named, scored or singled out.

## Guardrails

- The one rule for this pack: "Claude drafts the plans, nudges and reports. Managers do the coaching, and a person makes every call about someone's skill." This brief describes what a group does and why. Whether any one person can do the task is a call for their manager to make with them, and the verdict is a recommendation for the requester and L&D to agree.
- Personal data: under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, scores, observation notes and performance data about a named person are personal data. Work with roles, teams and totals, anonymise or aggregate anything before pasting it in, and follow the ICO's guidance and your organisation's AI policy.
- Fairness: if the gap is bigger in one site, shift or team, look for the cause in the work there (the rota, the tools, the induction, what the manager asks for), and never infer or mention protected characteristics. Where disabled staff are affected, consider reasonable adjustments to tools, process and training under the Equality Act 2010.
- Safety and regulated work: for safety-critical or regulated tasks (the Health and Safety at Work etc. Act 1974 and your sector's regulator), the brief can recommend training and other fixes. Decisions about who is competent stay with a qualified person under your organisation's procedure.
- Law: check the current versions of the laws and guidance named here. This is a draft, never legal advice.
- No invented facts: figures, targets, dates and causes come from the user or stay as placeholders or "to test".
